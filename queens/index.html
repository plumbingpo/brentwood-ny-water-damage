Typical Water Damage Problems in Queens

Starting at the top of a home and working toward the ground is a handy way to think about water damage. Our crews in Queens follow that same path on an inspection, and these five stops cause the most trouble.

Attics: The top of the house takes on water when shingles are damaged or the seal around a vent pipe gives out. Because insulation absorbs so much, a leak overhead may run through several storms before a mark appears below. Wet insulation stops insulating and keeps the wood beneath it damp. Our part is removing what is saturated and drying the wood, while a roofing contractor closes the opening.

Bathrooms: Bathroom floods usually come from a forgotten bathtub, a stopped-up sink or a toilet that will not quit rising. Even a tiled floor lets water pass at the grout, the tub edge and the base of the walls. In an upstairs bathroom the next stop is the ceiling of the room underneath. If the toilet bowl was the source, assume the water is unsanitary and have the area disinfected.

Walls: Supply lines and drain pipes run inside the walls, and a slow leak there can go on for a long time. You might see trim pulling away, a discolored spot spreading or drywall that gives when pressed. On these calls in Queens, an infrared camera and a pin meter show us how far the dampness reaches. Only that section is opened and dried, once a plumber has stopped the leak.

Tanks: A water heater holds a large volume under pressure, usually in a closet, garage or utility room. Drips from the relief valve, the drain valve or the fittings on top are early signs that parts are wearing. If the tank lets go, shut the cold inlet valve over the heater and turn off its power or gas supply. Our Brentwood Restoration crew extracts the water and checks the nearby walls and flooring, which often get wet from behind.

Drains: A clogged sewer line makes itself known at whichever drain sits lowest. Dark water rising in a floor drain or a ground-level shower means the main line is not carrying waste away. That water is Category 3, the most contaminated kind, and it takes protective equipment to clean up safely. Hold off on flushing or running taps, and keep the household clear while our team heads to Queens.

How Drying Works on a Queens Water Damage Job

A common question from Queens customers is what to expect once the machines are running. The first day covers extraction, taking out unsalvageable materials, and placing air movers and dehumidifiers where the water went. The equipment is loud, about like several large fans, and it makes the room warm. It has to run day and night, so please do not switch it off, move it, or open windows unless we ask you to.

Each day after that, a technician visits to take moisture readings in the same spots and record them. As areas dry, we move machines to the places that are lagging and take away the ones no longer needed. You will see the numbers and hear what they mean.

A typical job takes 3 to 5 days of drying. It can run longer when water sat for a while before it was found, when it reached insulation or hardwood, or when several floors are involved.
