No part of restoring a water-damaged building matters more than structural drying, yet it is also where untrained crews make the most mistakes. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

The structural drying technicians at Brentwood Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Daily monitoring is built into how we dry a structure, and it relies on calibrated meters, thermal cameras and thermo-hygrometers. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials
  • Address any water intrusion immediately, because the faster drying begins, the better the outcome
  • Keep belongings off the machines and out of the path of the air movers
  • Do not turn off professional drying equipment to reduce noise, because every hour matters
  • Shut the doors and windows of any room under drying, which keeps dehumidification working well
  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them

Warning Signs for Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Trapped moisture often announces itself with a persistent musty odor after cleanup
  • No visible water left, yet walls or floors still feel cold and damp
  • Drywall that is soft, swollen or shows moisture staining
  • Signs of deterioration where an old leak was left to dry on its own
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, peels or blisters after a leak
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • Condensation forming on windows in the area that was wet

The Benefits of Professional Structural Drying

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

The equipment used for professional structural drying is fundamentally different from consumer products. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.
