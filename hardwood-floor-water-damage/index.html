Hardwood Floor Water Damage Help in Brentwood

Hardwood responds fast when it gets wet. Boards take on moisture from below and from the edges, swell against each other and start to cup, with the edges rising higher than the center. Cupping alone is no reason to give up on the floor. Boards of solid wood often return to flat if they are dried during those early days. What matters is removing moisture from the wood and from its subfloor steadily and under control.

Our tool for hardwood is the floor drying mat. The mats are sealed to the surface and connected to a vacuum unit that draws moisture up through the boards and seams, while dehumidifiers keep the room air dry. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. Hold off on the sander until every board is fully dry. A cupped floor sanded early ends up crowned afterward.

Signs to Look For

  • Baseboards or door trim swelling where the floor meets the wall
  • Nail heads and seams ringed by dark staining
  • Cupping, where each board's edges rise above its middle
  • Boards shrinking apart and leaving gaps following a rushed dry out
  • Boards lifting off the subfloor or tenting upward at the seams
  • New squeaks or popping sounds when you walk across the wet area
What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Cracks From Rushed Drying

High heat or too much airflow pulls moisture from the surface faster than the core can follow. Lasting gaps and split or cracked boards are the usual outcome.

Rusting Fasteners

Lingering moisture rusts the fasteners, which then hold the boards less firmly. It can leave black staining around each fastener that goes deeper than a sanding reaches.

Water That Keeps Moving

Moisture beneath flooring does not stay put. It can reach under cabinets, wall plates and into neighboring rooms, or drip through to the ceiling of the level beneath.

Wet Subfloor

The subfloor can hold moisture long after the boards feel dry. Leave it wet and the cupping returns or mold grows up from below.

What We Do

How We Handle Hardwood Floor Water Damage

1

Take Baseline Readings

Our team meters the wet boards, the subfloor under them and a dry area of floor to compare against. From those numbers we learn the depth of the water and if the floor can realistically be saved.

2

Remove Surface Water

Standing water is extracted right away and rugs and furniture are moved off the wet area. Cutting off the water entering the joints early keeps swelling down.

3

Install the Mat System

A vacuum system hooked to mats sealed on the surface lifts moisture out of the boards from above. The room is managed with dehumidifiers so drying stays even and the boards do not crack.

4

Track Progress and Report

We track readings every day or two until boards and subfloor match dry reference areas. At that point you hear from us if it needs nothing, some replacement boards, or a refinish.

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Goes Under

Water slips through the seams and spreads between the boards and subfloor. The surface may wipe dry while the underside stays soaked.

24 to 48 hours

Cupping Starts

Swelling sets in and board edges lift. You might notice haze in the topcoat. The bare underside of the boards and the subfloor are where mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Pressure Builds Up

Expanding boards push harder and harder on one another. With fasteners losing their hold and boards compressing at the edges, some areas can lift away from the subfloor.

Past one week

Permanent Damage

Buckling, black staining and mold under the boards become likely. By now replacement, not drying, is the answer for most of the affected section.
